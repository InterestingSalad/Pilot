Why Does the CFS Even Exist?
Before you learn a single line item, understand this one idea. Everything else follows from it.
The Core Truth: A company can be profitable on paper and dead in reality. The Income Statement runs on accrual basis — it records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of whether cash has moved. The CFS is the truth-teller. It shows you what actually happened to real money.
🏪 The Simple Shopkeeper Analogy
Imagine a shopkeeper sells ₹10 lakh of goods in a year — all on credit. No customer has paid yet. His Income Statement shows Profit = ₹10 lakh. His Cash Flow Statement shows CFO = −₹2 lakh (he paid rent, salaries, suppliers in cash). He is profitable but illiquid. He cannot pay his staff next month. This is why Enron, Satyam, and IL&FS all had great P&Ls right before they collapsed. Cash doesn't lie. Profit can.
📚 What Ind AS 7 Says (The Law Behind the Statement)
Ind AS 7 (Indian Accounting Standard 7) — India's version of IAS 7 — mandates the Cash Flow Statement for all companies. Key provisions you must know:
  • CFS is a mandatory financial statement (along with Balance Sheet, P&L, and Notes)
  • Cash flows classified into exactly 3 categories: Operating, Investing, Financing
  • CFO can be reported by Direct or Indirect method (entity's choice)
  • "Cash" includes cash + cash equivalents (investments maturing within 3 months)
  • Bank overdraft repayable on demand = part of cash management, not financing
🔑 The Three Questions CFS Answers
CFO
Can the core business generate cash to sustain itself?
CFI
How much is being invested for future growth vs. divested?
CFF
How is the company funding itself — debt, equity, or returning capital?
CA Kalyanaraman's Rule #1: "Always read CFO first. A company with consistently negative CFO is living on borrowed time — literally and figuratively. No matter how pretty the P&L looks."
The 3-Section Structure
The CFS is exactly three buckets. Not two, not four — three. Master the classification rules and 80% of your exam is done.
The Master Rule for Classification:
Operating = day-to-day business | Investing = buying/selling long-term assets | Financing = funding the business (debt + equity)

⚠️ The 4 Flexible Items — Exam Favourite!
Interest received, Dividend received, Interest paid, Dividend paid — these can go in different sections under Ind AS 7. The company must choose a policy and apply it consistently. In GAAP (US), interest paid always goes in CFO. Ind AS gives flexibility. This difference is tested constantly.
🎯 The Classification Shortcut
Ask yourself 3 questions in order:
  1. Is it related to the core business / revenue generation? → Operating
  2. Is it buying or selling a long-term asset or investment? → Investing
  3. Is it raising or returning capital (debt or equity)? → Financing
If still confused — it's Operating. Residual category = Operating.
Operating Activities (CFO)
The most important section. The most tested section. The most misunderstood section. Let's fix that permanently.
CA Kalyanaraman: "CFO is the heartbeat. All other sections are noise if the heart is not beating. A company can survive years of negative CFI (it's investing). It can survive some CFF outflows (it's repaying). But it cannot survive negative CFO year after year. That's a corpse that hasn't fallen yet."
What Goes into CFO?
Everything related to earning revenue and incurring expenses from the primary business:
  • Cash receipts from sale of goods/services
  • Cash payments to suppliers and employees
  • Cash received from royalties, fees, commissions
  • Tax payments (unless specifically Investing or Financing)
  • Insurance claims receipts (operating nature)
  • Changes in working capital (receivables, inventory, payables)
Key Insight — Why WC Changes Matter: When you sell on credit, you've "earned" revenue but haven't received cash. That difference sits in Receivables. An increase in Receivables = cash stuck in the system = subtract from CFO. This is the most common exam calculation.
Direct Method: Show actual cash receipts and payments. Start from cash, not profit. Transparent but rarely used in practice (companies don't want to reveal exact cash collections).
Direct Method — CFO Template
Cash received from customers + Sales + Opening Debtors − Closing Debtors
Cash paid to suppliers − COGS + Closing Inventory − Opening Inventory + Closing Creditors − Opening Creditors... wait, this gets complex. That's why no one uses it.
Cash paid to employees − Salary expense ± outstanding salary changes
Tax paid − Actual cash paid to tax authority
= Net CFO = The cash heartbeat number
// Key formula for Direct Method
Cash from Customers = Sales − ΔDebtors (increase)
// If debtors increase → less cash collected than sales
// If debtors decrease → more cash collected than sales
Indirect Method: Start from Net Profit, then adjust for (1) non-cash items, (2) non-operating items, (3) working capital changes. This is what 95% of companies use. Master this and you master the exam.
Indirect Method — The Complete Template
Net Profit Before Tax START From P&L. Before tax, before extraordinary items.
STEP 1: Add back non-cash charges These reduced profit but didn't use cash
Depreciation & Amortisation + Non-cash. Reduces profit but zero cash out.
Provision for bad debts + Non-cash charge. Add it back.
Loss on sale of asset + Non-operating loss. Belongs in CFI.
Impairment loss + Non-cash write-down. Add back.
STEP 2: Remove non-operating income/expense These belong in CFI or CFF, not here
Interest income − Non-operating income. Goes to CFI.
Gain on sale of asset − Non-operating gain. Goes to CFI.
Dividend income − Non-operating. Goes to CFI or CFO (policy).
Interest expense + Non-operating expense. Goes to CFF.
= Operating Profit Before WC Changes SUB Subtotal — pure operating picture
STEP 3: Working Capital Changes The cash timing differences
Increase in Trade Receivables − Earned but not collected. Cash stuck.
Decrease in Trade Receivables + Collected more than earned this period.
Increase in Inventory − Cash paid for stock not yet sold.
Decrease in Inventory + Sold off old stock — cash positive.
Increase in Trade Payables + Bought but not paid. Cash conserved.
Decrease in Trade Payables − Paid suppliers. Cash gone.
= Cash Generated from Operations SUB Before tax payment
Income Tax Paid − Actual cash paid, not just provision
= NET CASH FROM OPERATING ACTIVITIES ✓ The heartbeat number (A)
The Working Capital Rule — Never Mess This Up Again:
Think of it as water in a pipe. An increase in a Current Asset = water trapped = SUBTRACT. An increase in a Current Liability = water borrowed from supplier = ADD. Decrease does the opposite.
ItemChangeEffect on CFOIntuition

Receivables / DebtorsDecrease ↓Add (+)Collected old dues — extra cash in
Inventory / StockIncrease ↑Subtract (−)Bought more stock — cash used
Inventory / StockDecrease ↓Add (+)Sold old stock — cash recovered

Memory Trick — "LAIR Rule":
Liability increases = Add | Asset increases = Remove (subtract)
Current Asset ↑ → subtract | Current Liability ↑ → add
Current Asset ↓ → add | Current Liability ↓ → subtract
Investing Activities (CFI)
Buying and selling the long-term infrastructure of the business. This section tells you if a company is building for the future or selling off its bones.
CA Kalyanaraman: "CFI is the strategy section. A growing company has large negative CFI — it's buying land, machinery, patents. A dying company has positive CFI — it's selling everything to survive. Context is everything."

⚠️ Critical Trap — Sale of Asset Treatment:
When an asset is sold, the full sale proceeds appear in CFI as inflow. The gain or loss is removed from CFO (in indirect method). Many students double-count.

Asset Book Value ₹100, sold for ₹130 → Gain = ₹30 (P&L)
In CFS: Remove ₹30 gain from CFO (subtract), Add ₹130 proceeds in CFI ✓
Capex vs. Revenue Expenditure: Only capital expenditure (Capex) appears in CFI. Revenue expenditure (repairs, maintenance) stays in CFO. The line between them is a major accounting judgment — and a major exam topic.
Financing Activities (CFF)
How the company funds its existence. Equity holders and debt holders — this section is all about them.
CA Kalyanaraman: "CFF tells you the capital story. Is the company growing its balance sheet by raising money? Or is it mature, giving money back to providers of capital? A startup has positive CFF. A blue-chip company has negative CFF. Neither is bad — context determines everything."

Issue of equity shares / IPO / Rights issueInflow +Proceeds net of issue costs
Share buyback / repurchaseOutflow −Return of capital
Issue of debentures / bondsInflow +Debt raised
Repayment of debenturesOutflow −Debt returned
Borrowings from bank (long-term)Inflow +Term loans
Repayment of bank loansOutflow −Debt serviced
Interest paidOutflow − (if policy = CFF)Cost of debt capital
Dividend paid (equity)Outflow − (if policy = CFF)Return to equity holders

Lease liability repayment (Ind AS 116)Outflow −Principal portion of lease = CFF
Ind AS 116 (Leases) — New Exam Topic: Under Ind AS 116, a lessee recognises a Right-of-Use asset and Lease Liability. In the CFS:
• Principal repayment of lease = CFF (Outflow)
• Interest on lease = CFF or CFO (entity's policy)
• Short-term and low-value leases = CFO (treated as operating expense)
⚠️ Short-Term Borrowings — Watch the Treatment:
Short-term borrowings (working capital loans, cash credit) can be shown net (net change) rather than gross inflows and outflows, if turnover is quick. Long-term must be shown gross.

Key Cash Flow Ratios
These are the ratios a credit analyst, equity analyst, or examiner will test. Know the formula, know the interpretation, know the threshold.
RatioFormulaGood / Bad ThresholdWhat it Measures
Free Cash Flow (FCF) CFO − Capex Positive = good Cash left after maintaining/growing assets. The "true" surplus.
CFO to Net Profit CFO ÷ Net Profit × 100 ≥80% = high quality Earnings quality. Are profits backed by real cash?
Capex Coverage CFO ÷ Capex > 1x = self-funded Can ops fund investments without external money?
Cash Interest Coverage (CFO + Interest Paid) ÷ Interest Paid ≥3x = comfortable Can ops service debt? Key for credit rating.
CFO to Sales CFO ÷ Net Sales × 100 Higher = better How much cash per rupee of sales? Operating efficiency.
Cash Flow Per Share CFO ÷ No. of Shares Compare to EPS Cash EPS. Higher than accounting EPS = high quality earnings.
Debt Coverage (CFF) CFO ÷ Total Debt > 0.2 = adequate How quickly can ops repay total debt?
Reinvestment Ratio Capex ÷ (Depreciation + Amortisation) > 1 = growing >1 means adding assets faster than they depreciate. Growth signal.
Accrual Ratio (Advanced):
Accrual Ratio = (Net Income − CFO) ÷ Average Net Assets
Higher positive accrual ratio = more earnings not backed by cash = higher manipulation risk. Satyam, DHFL, Nirav Modi's companies all had high accrual ratios before their frauds were exposed. This is the forensic accounting tool.
⚠️ Exam Traps & Common Mistakes
10,000 years of watching students make the same mistakes. Here they are — all of them — so you never join that list.
CA Kalyanaraman: "These are the traps I have set myself when I was examiner for 200 years. I know exactly where students fall. Memorise this list."
🪤 Trap 1 — The Asset Sale Double-Count
Asset sold for ₹150 (book value ₹100, gain ₹50).
Wrong approach: Put ₹50 gain in CFO, put ₹150 in CFI → double-counts ₹50.
Right approach: Remove ₹50 gain from CFO (subtract), put FULL ₹150 proceeds in CFI.
The full proceeds always go in CFI. The gain/loss is adjusted out of CFO.
🪤 Trap 2 — Depreciation is NOT a Source of Cash
Students write "Depreciation provides funds." Wrong. Depreciation is a non-cash charge that we ADD BACK to profit to remove its effect. It doesn't provide cash — it never created a cash outflow in the first place (the cash left when the asset was purchased). We only add it back because it was subtracted in computing profit.
🪤 Trap 3 — Tax Paid ≠ Tax Provision
Income tax provision (in P&L) = accounting charge based on estimated liability. Income tax paid (in CFS) = actual cash payment to tax authority in that period. They are different numbers. Always use "tax paid" — typically found in Notes or Tax Account.
🪤 Trap 4 — The Flexible 4 Items
Interest received, Dividend received, Interest paid, Dividend paid — each can go in different sections depending on company policy under Ind AS 7. Examiners will test this by asking "where can X be classified?" The answer for all four is: it depends on the entity's accounting policy, applied consistently.

Common exam policy setup: "Interest received → CFI, Interest paid → CFF, Dividend received → CFI, Dividend paid → CFF"
🪤 Trap 5 — Bank Overdraft Classification
Bank overdraft repayable on demand = part of Cash and Cash Equivalents, NOT a financing activity. This is counterintuitive because it's a liability, but Ind AS 7 treats it this way when it's used as part of day-to-day cash management. Overdraft with fixed repayment = Financing.
🪤 Trap 6 — FD Maturity Classification
Fixed Deposit with maturity ≤ 3 months = Cash Equivalent (part of cash, NOT investing activity). FD with maturity > 3 months = Investing activity. The 3-month rule is exact. Don't confuse with current assets on balance sheet.
🪤 Trap 7 — Bonus Shares / Stock Dividends
Bonus shares are issued from reserves — zero cash transaction. They do NOT appear in the Cash Flow Statement at all. They are disclosed as a non-cash transaction in Notes to Accounts.
Same for: Conversion of debentures to equity, acquisition via share swap.
🪤 Trap 8 — The Indirect Method Starting Point
Always start with Net Profit BEFORE Tax (not after tax, not after extraordinary items). Tax is deducted separately as "Tax Paid." Extraordinary items (if any) are shown separately. Starting from post-tax profit = guaranteed wrong answer.
🪤 Trap 9 — Lease Under Ind AS 116
Under old treatment: operating lease payments = CFO. Under Ind AS 116:
• Principal repayment of lease liability → CFF
• Interest on lease → CFF or CFO (policy)
• Short-term/low-value leases (exempted) → CFO
This shifts cash from CFO to CFF, improving CFO appearance without operational change.
🪤 Trap 10 — Foreign Currency Cash Flows
Cash flows in foreign currency are converted at the exchange rate at the date of the transaction (or average if approximately equal). The effect of exchange rate changes on cash held in foreign currency is shown separately — it is NOT part of CFO, CFI, or CFF. It's a reconciling item called "Effect of exchange rate changes on cash."
